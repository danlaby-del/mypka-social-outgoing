  Here's the mechanism: researchers tracked 15 baseball and softball batters through the identical pitch, twice. Once with a real partial swing. Once with no swing at all, just watching and predicting out loud where the ball would cross the plate.

  Everything about the pitch was the same. The only variable was whether the body was actually going to do something about it.

  When just watching, batters made predictive eye jumps, saccades, on 53% of pitches. When actually swinging, that dropped to 39.7%. Statistically significant.

  The eyes aren't a passive camera feeding a decision made somewhere else. They're wired into the action itself, and they change strategy the instant the action becomes real.
