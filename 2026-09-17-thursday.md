facebook_text: |
  Here is the part I have to be honest about.

  The obvious question with noise-based training is who benefits. In 2008, a group in Tokyo went after it elegantly, sending a faint signal to one eye and noise to the other so the two could only combine centrally in the visual cortex. Across 21 observers the gains varied enormously, and the variation was predicted by each person's own internal neural noise. The quieter the system, the larger the benefit.

  I have cited that finding for years.

  Then in late 2025 a group in Ankara asked the same question with 149 participants in the Journal of Neuroscience, and reported the opposite. The gains were most pronounced in those with the lowest baseline sensitivity.

  Two careful studies. One question. Opposite directions. I cannot pretend to resolve that for you.

  It gets harder. In 2023, an individually optimized noise dose replicated when retested in the same session, but not in a separate session. If Monday's optimal dose is not Tuesday's, nobody selling a fixed per-athlete setting is standing on the evidence.
