facebook_text: |
  Why would a nervous system work this way? It seems like interference should always cost you.

  The answer sits at the level of a single neuron.

  A neuron does not report gradually. It stays below a firing threshold and says nothing, then crosses that threshold and fires. It is a go or no-go device, which is why this maps so cleanly onto sport.

  Now picture a neuron carrying a signal almost, but not quite, strong enough to fire. Under clean conditions it never gets over, and you never perceive what it was trying to tell you. Add a little random fluctuation and, on some of them, weak signal plus noise clears the threshold. The neuron fires.

  Noise carries no information of its own. It lends the real signal just enough of a push to clear the gate.

  And a neuron sitting far below threshold, with nothing meaningful arriving, is not helped at all. The push only matters if you were already close.

  → sportsvision.nyc
