facebook_text: |
  The coaches in this survey didn't get to vision training by reading the research. The study's authors put it plainly: adoption was "practice-driven rather than theory-driven."
  
  I don't read that as a knock on coaches. Coaches are extraordinary observers of human performance — they've watched enough athletes to know the great ones "see the game" better, even if they've never opened a vision-science paper.
  
  So they reach for the tools. Strobe glasses. Reaction balls. Light boards. The market has exploded.
  
  But without a framework — without knowing what specific perceptual skill a tool actually targets — the training stays diffuse. No way to individualize it. No way to measure it. No way to know if the athlete who still can't read the play is vision-limited, attention-limited, or something else entirely.
  
  See this week's SPORTS VISION RADIO podcast for a deeper dive.
