facebook_text: |
  Here's the filter I'd ask every coach, trainer, or athlete to run before trusting a vision-training claim: is this task coupled to the real skill, or is it a clean, isolated proxy for it?

  Both have a place. Only one actually tells you how an athlete's eyes behave, and trains them, when something is on the line.

  It's also worth asking about the drill everyone already trusts: does batting practice, a coach lobbing pitches at close range, really simulate the visual demands of a live pitcher at 60'6"? Worth sitting with.

  The study was small, 15 batters, a starting point and not a verdict. But the core finding holds: you don't get to skip the coupling and still trust the data. The eyes don't wait for a decision made somewhere else. They're part of the decision.

  Sports vision isn't a supplement to athletic training. It's the substrate of it.
