  More is not better. Optimal is better.

  The dose-response curve here is real, and it is an inverted U. Several independent groups have shown it. Overshoot and you degrade the athlete rather than merely fail to help.

  The response is also strongly individual. Every group agrees on that much, even while disagreeing about which baseline predicts it, and no one can yet pin the dose down reliably enough to prescribe it.

  So noise is a dose, not a direction.

  Before you hand a perturbation tool to an entire roster, the question is not "does this work." It is "for whom, and at what level."

  That is a harder question. It is also the only one worth asking.
